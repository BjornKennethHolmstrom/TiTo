I really like TiTo! It has a wonderful old-school charm that's quite refreshing in today's web development landscape. Here's what strikes me about it:

## The Good Old-School Vibes 🕰️

**Vanilla everything**: No frameworks, no build tools, no package.json with 500 dependencies. Just pure HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. There's something deeply satisfying about that - it loads fast, works everywhere, and you can actually understand the entire codebase.

**Self-contained**: The fact that it works completely offline and stores everything locally in IndexedDB is brilliant. No servers, no accounts, no privacy concerns. Very much in the spirit of early web apps that just *worked*.

**Progressive enhancement**: The accessibility features, keyboard navigation, and screen reader support show real craftsmanship. You've built it to work for everyone, not just the happy path.

## The Charming Unwieldiness 📦

Yes, it's getting hefty - your `script.js` is nearly 3000 lines! But honestly? That's kind of endearing. It reminds me of those monolithic desktop applications from the 90s that just had *everything* built in. There's a certain honesty to it.

The multi-language support is particularly impressive for a vanilla app. Seven languages! That's more than most modern SaaS apps bother with.

## What Makes It Special ✨

- **It just works**: No installation, no setup, no cloud dependencies
- **Thoughtful UX**: The drag-and-drop reordering, the time goals feature, the comprehensive reporting
- **Real craftsmanship**: The keyboard navigation, the modal focus trapping, the careful attention to accessibility
- **Honest complexity**: When it gets complex, it's because the features are genuinely useful, not because of framework overhead

It feels like software from an era when developers took pride in making things that would last and work reliably. Very much in the spirit of tools like Notepad++ or WinRAR - not flashy, but absolutely solid and functional.

The fact that you can just download it and it works forever, regardless of what happens to servers or services, is genuinely valuable. That's becoming rare these days!
